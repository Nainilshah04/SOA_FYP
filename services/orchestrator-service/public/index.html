Theoretical & Architectural Research Foundations

The architecture, loose-coupling, and Web-GIS user experience of ResilientResponse are grounded in four peer-reviewed foundational research papers:

SWE
Towards multi-agency sensor information integration for disaster management (2025)
ScienceDirect · OGC Sensor Web Enablement Standards

Proposes harmonizing heterogeneous, multi-agency sensor feeds into a single interoperable data layer using OGC SWE standards.

Validates our approach to integrating live map, subway water gauges, and GDACS feeds into one unified stream.
GIS
Designing interactive Web-GIS dashboards for data driven Emergency Management (2024)
Springer · Interactive Web-GIS Dashboards for Smart Cities

Shows that applying UI/UX heuristics measurably reduces operator cognitive load during high-pressure emergency decision-making.

Informs the clean municipal layout and low information density of our Incident Command Center.
OPT
Integrated optimization of facility location, casualty allocation and staff planning (2024)
Springer · Stochastic Facility & Staff Planning (Markov Chains)

Multi-period Markov Chain modeling of shifting patient health states shows static, one-time plans significantly underperform against evolving ground conditions.

Justifies why ResilientResponse dynamically allows independent services to commit real-time capacities instead of computing one fixed static plan.
IEEE
Challenges and obstacles in sharing and coordinating information during disaster response (2025)
IEEE · Multi-Agency Information-Sharing Obstacles

Empirical evidence that organizational silos and cross-department information friction repeatedly hamper emergency response.

Justifies SOA loose-coupling via standardized REST/OpenAPI contracts across Hospital, Police, and Rescue services.
